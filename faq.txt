================================================================
PRN / PER DIEM EMPLOYEES
================================================================

PRN means "as needed." PRN workers, PRN staff, PRN employees, 
and per diem employees all refer to the same type of employee 
at the hospital. PRN and per diem are interchangeable terms.

Common questions about PRN / per diem employees:
- What is PRN?
- What does PRN mean?
- What is PRN coverage?
- What benefits do PRN employees get?
- What insurance does PRN get?
- Does PRN get health insurance?
- What is per diem coverage?
- What benefits do per diem employees get?
- I am PRN, what benefits am I eligible for?
- I work as needed, what coverage do I have?

Answer: PRN employees (also called Per Diem employees) are 
eligible for the Prime Value Plan. Coverage begins on the 
first of the month following or coinciding with two months 
from their hire date. For complete details visit 
ehp.primehealthcare.com

BENEFITS EFFECTIVE DATE / WHEN DOES COVERAGE START
================================================================

Common questions about when benefits start:
- When do my benefits start?
- When does my coverage begin?
- When does my insurance kick in?
- When am I eligible for benefits?
- How long until my benefits are active?
- When does my health insurance start?
- How do I know when my coverage starts?
- Can you give me an example of when my coverage starts?
- What is my benefits effective date?
- When will I have health insurance?

CRITICAL BENEFITS START DATE RULES:

RULE 1 - Hired on the 1st day of the month ONLY:
Benefits start exactly 2 months later on the 1st.
Example: Hired June 1st → Benefits start August 1st
Example: Hired July 1st → Benefits start September 1st
Example: Hired August 1st → Benefits start October 1st

RULE 2 - Hired on ANY other day (2nd through last day of month):
Benefits start 3 months later on the 1st.
This applies to the 2nd, 3rd, 15th, 20th, 30th, 31st — 
any day that is NOT the 1st.
Example: Hired June 2nd → Benefits start September 1st
Example: Hired June 15th → Benefits start September 1st
Example: Hired June 30th → Benefits start September 1st
Example: Hired August 2nd → Benefits start November 1st
Example: Hired August 3rd → Benefits start November 1st
Example: Hired August 15th → Benefits start November 1st
Example: Hired August 31st → Benefits start November 1st

SIMPLE RULE TO REMEMBER:
- Hired on the 1st → benefits start 2 months later on the 1st
- Hired on any other day → benefits start 3 months later on the 1st

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID:
- Hired August 3rd is NOT October 1st. It IS November 1st.
- Hired August 15th is NOT October 1st. It IS November 1st.
- Hired August 31st is NOT October 1st. It IS November 1st.
- Hired June 2nd is NOT August 1st. It IS September 1st.
- Hired June 15th is NOT August 1st. It IS September 1st.
- Only August 1st gives an October 1st start date.
- Only June 1st gives an August 1st start date.

HOW TO CALCULATE:
Step 1: Is the hire date the 1st of the month?
  - YES → add 2 months → that is the benefits start date
  - NO → add 3 months → that is the benefits start date
Step 2: The benefits start date is always the 1st of that month.
